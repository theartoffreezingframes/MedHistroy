Security center

Your Data. Your Control.

CareVault is designed around consent, not convenience shortcuts. Security is a set of behaviors, not a slogan.

🤝

Consent-based sharing

Nothing is shared until you explicitly approve a specific request.

⏳

Access expiration

Every grant has a time limit, enforced by the server.

🚫

Instant revocation

Revoke any active session; provider access is blocked immediately.

📜

Audit logs

Every request, view and revocation is recorded and visible to you.

🔐

Secure authentication

Hashed credentials and role-based sessions.

👥

Role-based access

Patients, providers and admins each see only what they should.

🔍

Source traceability

AI summaries always point back to original documents.

🛡️

Encrypted communication

Transport-layer encryption for all client–server traffic.

CareVault is a consent and experience layer. It is not a diagnostic system and does not make medical decisions.

Assisted access

No smartphone? No problem.

For patients with limited digital access, a provider can open Assisted Patient Access. The patient's identity is verified, the patient explicitly approves on screen, and temporary authorized access is granted — the patient still controls everything.

  • 1
    Identity check
    Name or ABHA number confirms the right patient.
  • 2
    Explicit approval
    The patient taps "I approve" — nothing is shown before.
  • 3
    Temporary access
    Time-limited and revocable, like every CareVault session.

Platform capabilities

A last-mile experience layer for digital health.

CareVault is designed to work alongside ABDM/ABHA infrastructure — never to replace it. It's the human layer on top: organization, verification, consent and continuity.

🇮🇳 ABHA-ready by design

Profiles can link an ABHA number. The backend is structured so live ABDM integration can be plugged in when credentials are available. The demo uses a clearly-labeled mock integration.

📶 Built for real networks

Lightweight pages, compressed responses and a connection indicator keep CareVault usable on slow mobile networks — where much of healthcare actually happens.
